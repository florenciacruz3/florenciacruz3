# What Writing a Memoir at 25 Is Teaching Me About My Technical Writing  

*The unexpected connection between creative and professional writing*

I’ve been working on a memoir about me and my struggles for several years now. But a lot has changed since then. 

I took a different path I thought I never would: technical writing. And, over time, I started noticing how much creative writing and technical writing have in common. 

Here are a few of my ideas on why I think technical writing and creative writing can go together:

---

**1.	What it’s like writing my memoir at the age of 25 and what it was like then**

I started writing this memoir at the age of twenty-two, left it aside for a couple of years, and came back to it now. 

I have to admit, reading something I wrote when I was only twenty-two made me cringe a little bit. And that got me thinking how your levels of maturity can reflect in your writing.  

We are talking about a personal memoir about struggles. There are a lot of feelings involved, suffering, pain, joy, and personal growth. 

And same goes with all kinds of writing. I went back and looked at my old papers from school, and they were terrible. 

I used to think that I had wasted so much time working in customer service instead of doing what I’m passionate about. But now, I’m starting to feel grateful that I did because it gave me time to grow. 

---

**2.	Whoever told you that creative writing and technical writing don’t match is wrong**

I’ve heard my mentor, @Miri Rodriguez, saying this to me once, and it gave me a new, whole perspective of what being a technical writer really meant for me. 

Once I started reviewing her work more closely, I started noticing how much her writing differs from other writers' writing. 

There's a lot more that goes into it than just writing a technical, informative, and objective document. There’s the voice of narration, your own personal experiences, human empathy, that no one can replace, that is all you. 

I used to think the same thing about writing non-fiction. It limits your style of writing compared to having the whole freedom that fiction gives you. 

But I was wrong. There’s so much that you can do, whether you decide to choose one word or another can change the whole meaning of your writing. And this applies both to technical writing and creative writing. 

Choosing to narrate your story in first, second, or third person, past tense, or present tense, changes everything. 

---

**3.	Voice of narrator**


I started noticing, while going back to my memoir, that you can clearly see my voice of narration changing throughout the book. 

While following along my personal growth, you can see changes in the level of maturity that I had and have now, how I used to think of a certain situation back then, and what I think about it now. 

After reviewing Miri’s work and even watching some of her interviews, I started noticing how her voice of narration also comes through. She uses her storytelling technique, which I didn’t think was possible to apply to technical documents, until I saw it. 

---

**4.	The human voice AI is not able to convey**

For many years, I’ve struggled with the idea that I shouldn’t be a technical writer because AI will someday replace us. 

I also used to think that AI wasn’t something you could use for creative writing and could only be used in technical writing. 

Later on, I learned how to use AI as a thinking tool, not a shortcut. AI has always been great at editing for grammar, improving layouts, or brainstorming ideas. However, AI will always be missing the human aspect of it: the use of empathy, personal experience, your own narrator voice, emotions. 

And the same goes with my memoir. AI doesn’t know how I felt at the time, what it meant to me, why I did certain things, and what goes in my train of thought. 

Miri has made it clear that the reason why storytelling is so important is because it attracts the reader, and that’s because the reader can find some way to relate to what has been said. 

Without this human element, all we have left are written pieces no one wants to read. 

---

**The Outcome**

When I was completing my bachelor’s degree in creative writing from the @University of Washington, I was very obstinate about wanting to write fiction and nothing else. 

I couldn’t see back then what an amazing gift it is to try something new, something I never imagined doing before, and end up loving it. 

After months of hard work, taking countless certifications, and writing an entire portfolio from scratch, I'm now able to do what I've been wanting to do for so long: write.

I got offered to be a part of @Empressa as a technical writer, which is something I never thought I would stand a chance of doing. 

---

**Conclusion**

The misconception that you can't do anything in life with a college degree in English is completely mistaken.

I used to feel that way, and decided to work in retail and daycare, because somehow, that seemed like the right thing to do. And I hated it. 

If I could go back in time and give myself a little bit of advice, I would say, you’re not going to regret trying something and not liking it. But you will regret those years that you waited to follow your passions because you were too scared to try.

Forget about whatever excuse you’re telling yourself: start now.
