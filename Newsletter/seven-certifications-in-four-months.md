# What Seven Certifications in Four Months Actually Taught Me 

*Not what I learned from the content, but what I learned about how I learn*

**Know the “Why?” you’re doing this**

When I took my first certification @Google Developers, I thought that what I was doing was pointless. That certification involved only reading, and I used to think, how can I put this on my resume? Anyone can add it to their resume, and it doesn’t mean that you’ve accomplished anything. 

The first time that I took this certification, I didn’t do the exercises that it would ask you to do. @Gaston Cruz, my mentor, suggested that I go back to this certification and write them all down. I ended up completing a whole thirty-page document. 

That’s when I realized, if you want to learn, you’ll always find a way to do more. 

> Tip 1: Your attitude is the most important aspect on how you learn

**Having a schedule**

With longer certifications, having a schedule has been, from my experience, the best way to get things done. Without a schedule, you’ll always have the excuse to say, “later.” People would be asking you, “When are you going to get certified? It’s been months.” And the answer would remain the same. 

There’s another side to it: pretending like doing a certification is a race, and you are in competition with yourself. With this, I mean, setting high standards for yourself. If the certification typically takes one month to complete, try doing it in two weeks. 

When I completed three of my @Coursera Project Management certifications, I couldn’t wait one whole month to get it done. And this is exactly how I was able to complete these seven certifications in only four months. 

> Tip 2: Challenge yourself. Test your abilities. 

**Be curious but also have a goal in mind**

I started off with the idea that I wanted to be a technical writer, so as I said, I completed Google Developers for that. 

While looking for jobs in that field, I realized that I was lacking qualifications. Some of them required Canva experience or content design. Some of them didn't fit exactly as technical writing roles, but would involve knowledge and experience in accessibility, UX writing, and information architecture.

So, I went my own way to do some research and get a certification on @Canva Essentials and @Introduction to Web Accessibility. 

> Tip 3: It is natural to want to expect immediate results, but having the ability to pause and think, “How can I be more fit for the role I’m looking for?” is the kind of skill that sets you apart when it comes to recruitment. 

**Put these certifications into practice**

Looking back at that first certification experience, I learned that, even though people have different styles for learning, the actual skills that employers look for are, “Can you put this into practice?”

Once I completed each one of the mentioned qualifications, I wrote at the very least two pieces for each one of them. Not only do I have those seven certifications now, I also have a full portfolio to back it up. 

> Tip 4: Unless you’re constantly writing and updating your portfolio, the knowledge that you gained from your work will be forgotten. 

**Be open-minded**

Once I did enough research and practice on technical writing, my mentor decided that it might be a good idea to look into project management. 

At first, I was very disappointed that I had to stop working in technical writing and completely redirect my learning toward something that didn't seem relevant to me. 

The next day, I started my first certification, and ended up completely loving it. 

I learned about how we all have at some point in our lives project managed an event or trip. That got me thinking about the skills that I gained from managing my time to complete seven certifications in four months and how those skills translate directly into what project managers do. 

> Tip 5: You might regret not following someone’s advice or missing out on a job opportunity, but you’ll never regret giving something new a shot. Try it, you have nothing to lose. 

**Keeping a learning log**

Keeping a learning log is a great idea to get your foot in the door, and let the world know who you are. 

Having a long list of certifications and a portfolio is impressive, but it doesn’t really matter if no one knows that you’re doing this. 

Certifications aren’t just a list to put on your resume. You need to make people care. And once you do, every certification that you do moving forward will have a meaning. 

> Tip 6: Be transparent, and try to keep this in mind, the job of your dreams is not going to come looking for you. Networking, talking to the right people, making your name known is how opportunities become possible. 

**Be ready to be disappointed**

As I mentioned, I always like to be in control, I like having a schedule, knowing what I’m going to do next, where I’m going to be, what the plan is. But sometimes, plans can fall apart.

When I started preparing for project management, completing my long certifications as fast as I could, trying to make everything fit perfectly, I ended up failing the exam for it. 

And from then on, everything changed. Gaston and I decided to take a little break from project management and focus on a new project. I was offered an opportunity to be an account executive while taking some time to prepare for my exam at a slower pace. 

In the meantime, I was able to complete another certification, @HubSpot Inbound to get a better understanding of what the work as account executive would look like. 

> Tip 7: Be ready for the unexpected. Having discipline is important. But what's more important is knowing how to pick yourself up after unexpected changes happen.  

**The Outcome**

Four months ago, I entered this field expecting to take maybe two or three short certifications, get freelance work immediately for maybe a month, and move on into working as a technical writer for a company as big as @Microsoft. 

It was interesting to learn that life just doesn’t work that way. This is not a short process with immediate results. Learning, growing, and gaining new skills is a lifetime process. 

I now can say not only, “I have completed seven certifications and an internship,” but “I’ve grown and expanded within my abilities. I’ve gained the confidence that I was lacking four months ago to navigate complex tools and systems of the tech industry, and communicate them into clear, structured content that works for the reader. I’ve learned about hard work and thinking outside the box. Completing seven certifications and a portfolio wasn’t just an assignment that was given to me, it was something I needed to do in order to be who I am now.”

**Conclusion**

If you’re just starting now, remember, there is always more that you can do. 

When looking into expanding to a different field, you should know that the title is not always the main reason why you should be doing something, it is about what you are learning, who you are becoming, and the doors that open when you commit to showing up every day.
