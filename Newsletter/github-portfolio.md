# How I Built My GitHub Portfolio from Zero  

*The structure, the decisions, and what I would add if I started today*

I had no idea what building a portfolio would entail. I didn’t know what I was supposed to write about or why people should care about what I was writing. 

Before choosing this path, it was simple: I would create a zip file with essays that I had written for school. Generally, just three to four essays and it didn’t matter what topic it was. I would pick the best ones and send them to any job that would require written samples.

So, the first thing I did when I started working with @Gaston Cruz was learning what a portfolio really is, where to publish it, and what to write about. 

Here are some of my tips on how to get started: 

**Get certified**

When you are starting a new career path and you are new, you need to gain some basic knowledge on what you’re going to be doing. You wouldn’t write about a topic you don’t know anything about. And even if you did, it doesn’t hurt to reinforce or expand on that knowledge. 

I completed eight certifications, all about different fields and roles, and I wrote at least one or two papers on each one of them. I went from writing technical documents, writing about accessibility and UX writing, to writing about project management. 

> Tip 1: Always be open to doing more. Having articles from different roles will allow you to stand out from other candidates by showing them that there’s more you can do. 

**Get familiarized with other platforms**

I personally have been using @GitHub to store my writing samples. I had to learn how to write in Markdown syntax for that, which only took a couple of hours to do. 

From then on, everything that I’ve written has been stored in GitHub. Using Markdown syntax will make your life so much easier. No more worrying about layouts and pretty designs, or pasting texts and having your whole document shifting. 

Markdown will allow you to focus on what you should be focusing on: writing.

> Tip 2: It might seem scary and also a waste of time to learn how to use new platforms or different systems. I used to think that too when I first started, but if you just give it a couple of hours or an afternoon, you might find something that is not only practical, but turns out to be a much more professional platform than just a zip file with three Word documents on it. 

**Show us who you are**

After creating a professional, well-written portfolio that took you months to create, you’ll want other people actually reading it. 

The best way to do it for me was using @LinkedIn. Making connections, posting, commenting, creating a newsletter like I’m doing now, it's a great way to let the world know, “This is me, this is what I can do, and I’m open to doing more.” 

Before working with Gaston, I would be on LinkedIn applying for jobs for hours, but no one knew who I was back then. Making connections, networking, and knowing the right people, is how I got the opportunity of working as an account executive at @Informed Decision. And how I later got to meet @Miri Rodriguez and be offered an internship at @Empressa as a technical writer. 

> Tip 3: LinkedIn is much more than a platform for applying to jobs. There’s a lot more to it that comes with getting a job than just applying. Start showing your work to as many people as you can, not just recruiting managers.  

**The Outcome**

It turns out that it takes effort to build a portfolio. And once that effort is applied, the results can go beyond what you’ve expected. As I mentioned, I’ve completed eight certifications, written at the very least fourteen articles, worked on a real-life project, and was offered two internships. All in the course of four months. 

When people ask me, “What do you do for a living?” I usually don’t know where to begin. The conception that you’re just a job title and a company name doesn’t work for me anymore. 

**Conclusion**

If you’re just getting started and you find that most jobs require or recommend having a portfolio, don’t take the easy way out. That zip file with three essays from school has gotten me nowhere. 

Put some effort now, and you won’t regret it later. 

Recruiters look for people who are confident. Someone who can talk about their work without rambling or not knowing what to say. People who are able to defend their own work, explain in simple words what it is that they do, and show skills that they don't teach at school, are far more competitive in the workforce.
