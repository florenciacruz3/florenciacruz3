# What Working Under a Microsoft MVP Actually Looks Like Day to Day  

*The mentorship model, the feedback loops, and what makes it work*

I’ve always admired @Gaston’s work. Knowing that he stopped what he was doing, which was a bachelor’s degree in accounting, to chase after something he was actually passionate about. The tech industry was very slowly starting in Uruguay at the time Gaston entered it. There were no hopes, no prospects at the time, and Gaston was building this from scratch.

Seeing his progress from being new and not knowing what to do next to where he is right now, Microsoft MVP, was something I’ve always admired. 

So, when I got offered to work under his mentorship, you can imagine I was more than excited. 

Working under his mentorship has been amazing, both in a good way and a surprising way. 

Here are a few of our daily routines that I never imagined I would be doing. 

---

**Expanding my portfolio**

I came to Gaston with the goal of becoming a technical writer at @Microsoft. I thought all it would take would be just taking maybe three certifications and a few projects to do that. 

After we were done with that, we ended up moving into accessibility, UX writing, and information architecture.

And I didn’t know why at the time. 

---

**It takes time**

I expected to do this for a month, land that wonderful dream job I always wanted, and not have to do or worry about anything else ever again. 

But that’s not recommended, and that doesn’t guarantee that you’ll never have to worry about your career ever again. 

There are thousands of people being laid off every day. Following the plan that I made up in my head would probably lead me, five years from now, to have to find a job all over again. And all I would have left in my portfolio would be “Technical Writer at Microsoft.” No skills, no qualifications, and no real experience.

---

**You never know where you’ll end up**

As I said, I ended up expanding from technical writing into some related career paths. What I was not expecting was to move into project management out of nowhere and actually like it.

Project management was perfect for me, and I even considered it a better choice than technical writing for a while. 

But what I was lacking in all of this was experience. 

I got offered an internship at @Informed Decision as an account executive, which, again, had nothing to do with me. But the skills that I gained do: problem solving, organization, attention to detail, learning how to work with @HubSpot, and how to use AI in ways I haven’t before. 

And now, finally, I was offered to work for @Empressa as an intern in technical writing. 

---

**Asking questions is always allowed**

When I was working with Gaston, I completed a real-life project. It was an article called, “The Hidden Risks in Your Microsoft Fabric Tenant – and How to Find Them Before They Find You.” 

To start with, I had no idea what Microsoft Fabric, a Tenant Scan, Power BI, or Azure was at the time. But I had to write an article about it.

I might have said to Gaston at least ten thousand times: I don't get it, explain it again, remind me what this means, and the usuals: why, what, how.

And as a technical writer, it wasn't my job to be an expert in these sorts of things or have any prior knowledge of what migrating to Fabric meant.

But I needed to be able to explain it.

I learned throughout these past few months that to generate good writing, you have to understand everything there is about what you’re writing about. Once you understand it, writing just happens. 

---

**Routines can change**

At first, it was just Gaston and me. During the course of four months, we worked on getting eight certifications done, creating a written portfolio, doing a technical writing project, learning how to navigate @GitHub and write in markdown syntax, networking on LinkedIn, applying for jobs at Upwork. 

Once I started working with @Erik Johnson at Informed Decision, my workload started to change. I was dedicating around three to four hours to my internship and the rest to networking on LinkedIn and preparing for a Scrum exam.

And now that I’m working for @Miri Rodriguez, there will be only weekly check-ins with Gaston because I’ll mostly be busy doing both internships. 

But that doesn’t mean I will stop learning from him. 

---

**The Outcome**

The not knowing where I’m going to be used to terrify me. Now, I can finally feel excited about it. 

I started off thinking that I wasn’t good enough or smart enough to do this. After these four months, however, I realized that I’ve proven myself wrong. There are tons of skills, qualifications, experiences, and projects that I’ve built. And that’s only in the course of four months; I still have a long way to go. 

---

**Conclusion**

For those of you trying out mentorship, be aware that goals can change, and being open-minded is important. My idea of success used to be working at Microsoft as a technical writer, period. Now it is just doing what I love and being willing to try, whether it’s technical writing, project management, accessibility, you name it. 

But also, be mindful that if you find that whatever you're doing is not for you, it is your right to always say, no, I want to do something different.
