# How I Went from Daycare Teacher to Account Executive in Four Months

*The full story of the pivot, what it actually took, and what nobody tells you about starting over*

**Beginning Stage**

After graduating from The University of Washington with two bachelor’s degrees in English, and Cinema and Media Studies, I didn’t know what to do. So, I decided that I wanted to be an English teacher because that seemed like my only choice at the time. 

I picked these majors because I wanted to study in school something I would enjoy, not thinking about the prospects of what’s out there after those four years. 

> Tip 1: Look at the big picture. Enjoying your work is important, but also remember that college are only four years of your life. 

**Going into the actual battlefield: Starting your career**

After getting my bachelor's, most jobs in general would tell me I was lacking experience. So, I decided to get in the childcare business. 

I spent most of my life dreaming of going to college in the US and becoming someone. After I did, I learned skills such as argumentation, narrative structure and storytelling, cross-cultural communication, and problem-solving skills. Yet, there I was sweeping the floor, singing children’s songs, and changing diapers. But I told myself, this will pay off, someday I’ll be able to work as a high school teacher.  

After days and days of the same routine, I decided that I was tired of this. 

> Tip 2: It is true that to get a career you have to start from the bottom, but that doesn’t mean you have to stay in a job you hate for years.

**Reflecting on who do you want to be**

I did some reflecting and started thinking about what I could use my skills for. My focus in school was creative writing. I wanted to become a novelist, but unless I was planning on becoming the next J.K.Rowling, becoming famous and making millions of dollars, I would have to find the next best thing. 

But the truth is, I never really wanted to be a teacher. 

While working at the daycare, I was really hoping to simply get an office job, where I wouldn’t come home covered in sweat and having my legs hurting from picking up kids and running all through the classroom. So, finding some administrative work to do seemed like the right next step. 

I had previously worked in several customer service jobs (food service and retail). What I used to enjoy the most was learning technical terms and communicating them to people. 

> Tip 3: Be realistic but also have standards.

**Knowing the right people**

Months went by, and I kept getting rejected from every job that I would apply to due to lack of experience. 

But eventually, I got offered the opportunity to work under the mentorship of Gaston Cruz, Microsoft MVP in Data & AI platforms. I stopped working at the daycare completely and decided to dedicate all of my time to this entrepreneurship. 

We started off with technical writer. And after some practice using Markdown and GitHub, I managed to complete a project, “The Hidden Risks in your Microsoft Fabric Tenant — and How to Find Them Before They Find You.” 

Eventually, we expanded a little bit more into accessibility, UX writing, and IA. While doing this, I managed to complete eight certifications and continue building a portfolio on GitHub. We are currently in the process of getting some qualifications in Project Management. 

> Tip 4: Making connections is key to getting that first opportunity. Make sure to look for them. 

**The outcome**

About a month ago, I got introduced to Erik Johnson’s company at Informed Decisions through my mentor. As a starting point, I got offered the opportunity of working as an Account Executive for this company. 

It was overwhelming at first. I had to learn how to operate HubSpot’s CRM, use AI in ways I had never known before, learn about marketing and sales strategies. It took me about ten days to finally get the hang of it.

But thanks to the skills that I learned through working with Gaston, I was able to complete certifications, do my own personal research, and eventually adapt to this internship. 

I think, maybe the reason why it took me so many years after finishing my college degree to get serious about my work was because I was afraid I'd need to start at the beginning all over again. It seemed really intimidating. 

I was told growing up that what you do is, you get good grades in school so that you can get to college. You finish a four-year career, immediately apply to as many jobs as you can, and within a couple of months, you end up working in a corporate job. Sadly, this was not my experience. 

> Tip 5: Getting the experience is everything. Money is just a small portion of what having a job is. Be willing to wait, be patient, and the work that you do will pay off. 

**Conclusion**

If you’re currently enrolled in college and thinking that after graduating, you’ll immediately find the job of your dreams and everything will fit into your life, I can tell you myself from my experience, opportunities don’t just fall on your head. It’s about knowing the right people, putting in the work, going outside of the box, and going out of your way to get things. 

The advice that I wish I had listened to while I was still at school is, **STOP** waiting. Your career doesn’t start after you finish college. Your career starts now.
